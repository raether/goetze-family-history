# Gertraute Götze Fischer

*1895–1981 in the chronicle*

[Biography index](../README.md)

## Biography

Gertraute was Gutrune’s older sister. Gutrune remembers her writing poetry as a girl and going to the Leipzig Opera with their father. Gutrune says Gertraute trained in foreign-language business correspondence and worked in several German cities before joining the library of Berlin’s Altes Museum. Gutrune and their mother helped care for Gertraute’s son Franzl in Berlin. He died of diphtheria in 1934 at the age of seven. [Gutrune’s chronicle, Gertraute](../../records/chronicles/gutrune-1988-english.md#gertraute-18951981)

In her July 1937 application for an American Guild writing grant, Gertraute said she had worked for the Prussian state in Berlin until autumn 1935 and supported herself with typing work in New York. She wanted to publish as **Germaine Eduard** out of concern for her siblings in Germany. She submitted an unpublished novel about how Nazi rule intruded into the private lives of independent women. The archive says she received the grant. [Gertraute’s 1937 American Guild application (S8)](https://we-refugees-archive.org/archive/gertraute-goetzes-bewerbung-bei-der-american-guild-gertraute-goetze/)

Gutrune recalls that Gertraute met Walter Fischer while living in a New York boardinghouse with other European refugees. They later moved to California, worked in the San Francisco area, and built a small home near Oroville where they grew vegetables and fruit. Gutrune cared for Gertraute in her final years and records that she died of pneumonia on 18 November 1981. [Gutrune’s chronicle, Gertraute](../../records/chronicles/gutrune-1988-english.md#gertraute-18951981)

Gutrune identifies Gertraute by name as her sister. The 1938 manifest separately names a sister G. Fischer in New York, and the exile archive has a 1937 application under Gertraute’s name. A birth or marriage record remains a priority for confirming her parentage.
