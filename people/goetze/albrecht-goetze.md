# Albrecht Götze

*1897–1971*

[Biography index](../README.md)

## Biography

### Early life and scholarship in Germany

Albrecht Ernst Rudolf Götze was born in Leipzig on 11 January 1897. He began university studies in Munich in 1915, then served in the infantry during the First World War. His sister Gutrune recalls that he was wounded in the leg at the Battle of the Somme and wore an orthopedic shoe for the rest of his life. After the war he resumed his studies in Berlin and Heidelberg. [Gutrune’s chronicle][chronicle] · [Biographical overview][wikipedia] · [History of linguists in exile][exile]

At Heidelberg he earned a doctorate with a dissertation on how sounds changed in ancient Italic dialects. The history of linguists in exile and the Wikipedia overview give **1922** for the doctorate, while Yale’s finding aid gives **1923**. He taught at Heidelberg and edited Carl Bezold’s Babylonian-Assyrian glossary, published in 1926. His work on Hittite included editions of cuneiform texts excavated at Boğazköy, in present-day Türkiye. In 1930 he became a professor at the University of Marburg. One result of this work was his 1933 edition of the annals of the Hittite king Muršili II. [History of linguists in exile][exile] · [Annals catalogue][annals] · [Yale papers, p. 4][yale] · [Biographical overview][wikipedia]

### Leaving Germany and arriving at Yale

Gutrune remembers Albrecht as a Social Democrat who spoke out against the persecution of Jews. The history of linguists in exile records his public opposition to the Nazis and his removal from his Marburg post in 1933. He worked with the linguist Holger Pedersen in Copenhagen and spent time teaching in Oslo before going to Yale as a visiting professor in 1934. [Gutrune’s chronicle][chronicle] · [History of linguists in exile][exile] · [Yale papers, p. 4][yale]

A 1935 letter to Margret, written in Greensboro, Vermont, shows what the move meant for the family. Albrecht was tracking a shipment of furniture and arranging what to do with the things left in Europe. He also wrote about what he hoped scholars arriving from Europe could contribute to American life. He became a naturalized U.S. citizen in 1940. [Albrecht’s 1935 letter][letter] · [Yale papers, p. 4][yale]

### Yale, cuneiform studies, and Iraq

Albrecht joined Yale’s regular faculty in 1936, became a Sterling Professor in 1956, and retired in 1965. He worked with Edgar H. Sturtevant on *The Hittite Ritual of Tunnawi* (1938), an edition and translation of a Hittite ritual text. He directed the American School of Oriental Research in Baghdad and founded the *Journal of Cuneiform Studies*, whose first volume appeared in 1947. [Yale papers, p. 4][yale] · [Academic career profile][karttunen] · [Tunnawi book record][tunnawi] · [ASOR history][asor]

His research ranged from divination to law. In *Old Babylonian Omen Texts* (1947), he published a group of difficult tablets in Yale’s Babylonian Collection that record how ancient Babylonians interpreted omens. His work on the *Laws of Eshnunna* began with two tablets found at Tell Harmal, near Baghdad. He published an edition with an English translation in 1948 and returned to the text for a fuller edition in 1956. [Yale’s history of the collection][curators] · [Digitized *Old Babylonian Omen Texts*][omens] · [Study of the *Laws of Eshnunna*][law-history]

Gutrune recalls Albrecht telling stories of riding and dining with Arab hosts during his trips. A newspaper clipping kept by the family describes the Shammar naming him an honorary sheikh during a research trip in Iraq. It also shows him wearing the headcloth he received. Gutrune mentions that he later visited his brother Raimund in Venezuela. [Gutrune’s chronicle][chronicle] · [Family newspaper clipping][clipping]

### The Yale collection and his papers

With Yale curator Ferris J. Stephens, Albrecht helped document more than 3,000 cuneiform tablets held in public and private collections across North America. He served as curator of the Yale Babylonian Collection in 1962–1963. Yale’s account of its curators includes a portrait of him. [Yale’s history of the collection][curators]

Albrecht died on 15 August 1971 in Garmisch-Partenkirchen. His professional papers at Yale University Library include correspondence in German and English from 1923 to 1971. The finding aid lists the individual correspondents and gives box and folder numbers. His son Dieter also shared family research with Gutrune and received a copy of her chronicle in 1989.
