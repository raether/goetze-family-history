## English translation

### PDF page 3

```text
Stimson Cottage, Greensboro, Vermont
August 13, 1935
Dear Margret,
Many thanks for your letter. At least we know now that our belongings are on their way. We
knew from the start that the journey would involve some risks. We’ve gotten out of the habit
of getting upset over things like this.
Of course, it was completely against our agreement for Heimann to send the furniture in a
railway freight car instead of a moving van. Since it’s already happened, all I can do is give
notice now of any claims we may have for damages. I’ve done that. We’ll only be able to tell
what condition the furniture is in when we unpack it. I have no illusions about any of this.
We’ve known for a long time that people treat you shabbily, especially when you’re so far
away. The saying that all freight agents are scoundrels seems to have proved true once again.
Thank you for all the trouble you went to. I still hope that, despite everything, the shippers’
carelessness hasn’t entirely undone all the care you took with our things.
Please feel free to use whatever was left behind that might be useful to you. I’d only like to
reserve the right to take things back as needed in the very unlikely event that we return to
Europe. With that in mind, I’d like you to keep the black wardrobe and the matching bed
frame, even if you really can’t use them now. The children are growing up, after all, and need
room for their things.
As for the other things you can’t use and that are only taking up space, throw them out or give
them away. I doubt anyone would buy them now.
As for the bookshelves, I’m happy for Gottfried to buy them for his school. Let him weigh my
interests against those of the school and decide what price he thinks is fair. Under the
regulations in force, the money must go into my blocked account at the Dresdner Bank in
Berlin. Hermann is the best person to ask about those rules.
```

### PDF page 4

```text
I’m giving Gottfried the sheepskin as a keepsake forever. It was bought in Ankara and is
guaranteed genuine. Since I’m considered a wolf now anyway, I have no more use for it.
We’ll be unpacking before long. Since you’ve prepared us for the worst, there won’t be any
disappointments. Anything that turns up more or less intact will count as having been rescued
from the debacle. The belongings certainly won’t make us homesick. Fortunately or
unfortunately, depending on how you look at it, we’ve never felt that way. Since we’ve been
enjoying all the natural beauty up here in the northern United States, we no longer miss the
German landscape either. There’s more than enough here to satisfy everything we might long
for.
There are things we brought with us that we can never lose, things that perhaps don’t exist in
America. Everything else we find here can only enrich us. When this country takes in
intellectuals and scholars, it does so deliberately, in the hope that bringing together European
culture and the American outlook will produce something that moves America forward and
makes up, to some extent, for the lack of long-established traditions that is inevitable in a
young country. As I see it, anyone who enjoys this country’s hospitality and has been
welcomed with open arms has a duty to live up to that hope. In any case, circumstances leave
him no room to look back.
Warm greetings to Gottfried and the whole brood of children, who surely still remember their
uncle.
Your brother-in-law,
Albrecht
```
