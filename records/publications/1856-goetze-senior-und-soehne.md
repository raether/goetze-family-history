# Götze senior und Söhne in Glauchau

English translation of the trade profile published in the *Album der Sächsischen Industrie*, edited by Louis Oeser, vol. 1, Neusalza, 1856, pp. 149–150. The population, trade claims, and references to current conditions reflect the publication’s account in 1856.

## English translation

Glauchau, which we now enter, lies on the banks of the Mulde. With its two old castles, it is a beautiful, even romantic sight. Its factories and trade in manufactured goods place it among the most important towns in Germany. Within Saxony, it ranks among the foremost after Chemnitz.

The town now has 13,146 inhabitants, whose chief occupation is weaving woolen, part-wool, and part-silk fabrics. More than eight hundred master weavers carry on this work with their journeymen and apprentices. Many more masters in the surrounding towns and villages also work for Glauchau, particularly in Waldenburg and the Mülsen valley.

The fabrics made here compete successfully with English goods and even surpass them in quality. Glauchau is regarded as the true home of Saxon merino cloth.

Glauchau has numerous roads leading in all directions. Trade and travel will be made easier still by the railway from Chemnitz to Zwickau, which is expected to open soon and will have a station here.

Of Glauchau’s many establishments, we turn first to Götze senior and Sons. The firm is in the Chemnitzer Vorstadt district. Its premises consist of five buildings around an enclosed courtyard and a tastefully built residence. Gardens surround all the buildings.

The firm makes woolen, part-wool, and silk-blend fabrics and carries out textile finishing. Its goods are sold at home, abroad, and overseas. Leipzig is the only trade fair it attends. Its equipment includes a steam engine and three cylinder shearing machines used to trim the surface of cloth.

Twenty-five people are regularly employed in the factory and finishing buildings. The weaving itself is done off the premises, partly in Glauchau and partly in the surrounding area, where the firm provides steady work for a considerable number of weavers.

The owners are Carl Eduard Götze and Emil Herrmann Götze. The business was established at the beginning of this century under the name Götze Gebrüder (Götze Brothers). The present firm took its place in 1843.
